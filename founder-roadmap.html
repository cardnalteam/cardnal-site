02

The product goal

Cardnal turns a confusing rewards decision into a fast, explainable action without requiring sensitive financial access.

Product promise: tell Cardnal what you own and what you value. Cardnal figures out what to use and shows its work.

What Cardnal knows

  • The cards a user chooses to add
  • The merchant or category being considered
  • Public reward terms that Cardnal has verified
  • The user's stated goals and default card
  • How fresh and confident the underlying data is

What Cardnal refuses to pretend

  • It does not invent a rate when a rule is missing
  • It does not compare cash and points without an approved valuation
  • It does not guess a checkout total
  • It does not recommend cards the user does not own
  • It does not claim guaranteed rewards or savings

05

Path to public launch

Launch means the product can safely support early public users and the team can operate it when data, code, or user behavior goes wrong.

1. Product quality is proven

Supported queries resolve accurately. Unsupported or ambiguous queries abstain. Feedback has a clear owner.

2. The system is dependable

Extension permissions, local state isolation, service errors, monitoring, backups for supporting data, and rollback are tested.

3. The data operation exists

Every public rule has a source, reviewer, date, freshness policy, and correction workflow.

4. Users are protected

Privacy, terms, disclaimers, export, deletion, support, and clear product limitations are live.

5. The team is ready

We can answer support questions, disable a bad rule, recover an incident, and explain what Cardnal knows.

How the pieces connect

The same recommendation logic should serve the popup and future checkout panel. The interface changes. The decision contract does not.

Extension popup flow

Active tabtoPopuptoEnginetoRules + local wallettoExplained result

The popup reads the current tab context and local state. The engine makes the decision. The UI presents the result, alternatives, freshness, and abstention state.

Purchase-time flow later

Merchant pagetoExtensiontoEnginetoValidated totaltoSafe panel

Checkout injection is a later surface. It must never cover payment controls or show a guessed dollar amount.

Important boundary: the browser never receives the Supabase service-role key. User-owned data is protected by authentication and Row Level Security. Canonical reward data is not editable by normal users.

11

Decisions we must make deliberately

These are not hidden engineering assumptions. They are founder decisions because they shape cost, trust, and what Cardnal becomes.

What is the first self-serve surface?

Recommended: extension popup first, then a controlled checkout panel once the engine, selectors, and rules are stable. The web companion is required for account, card setup, wallet management, preferences, pairing, and privacy. It should not become a separate web recommendation workflow.

How broad is the first catalog?

Recommended: 10 to 20 deeply verified cards and 20 to 50 merchants or categories. More coverage is only better if the team can maintain it.

Which goals ship first?

Cash and points preferences are straightforward. Welcome bonuses, card offers, and best overall value require explicit user-declared data or a disclosed valuation model and should remain gated until their behavior is honest.

When do we consider bank connections?

Only after repeated usage proves the recommendation is valuable and evidence shows manual card setup is the primary adoption barrier. Bank access changes the security, legal, vendor, and support profile of the company.

Who owns reward-rule verification?

This must have a named owner and cadence. Reward data is a recurring editorial operation, not a one-time engineering import.

User states

  • Visitor: has not shared anything and needs a simple promise.
  • Waitlist member: has expressed interest and is a research candidate.
  • Signed in: has an account but has not completed setup.
  • Configured: has at least one card and can ask a supported question.
  • Recommending: is waiting for or viewing an answer.
  • Returning: has used the product before and should reach value faster.
  • At risk: has abandoned setup, rejected a result, or encountered an unsupported case.
  • Beta advocate: returns, gives detailed feedback, and refers a relevant user.

Internal states

  • Rules ready: current verified data is available.
  • Rules stale: a result may be shown with visible limits or suppressed.
  • Merchant unknown: no safe classification exists.
  • Result tied: no meaningful winner exists within the configured threshold.
  • Engine error: computation or rules loading failed.
  • Review required: a user report or source change needs an owner.
  • Release blocked: a gate is not satisfied and expansion should stop.
  • Incident mode: a bad rule or deployment needs rollback or disablement.

The required web companion owns account creation, card catalog search, wallet management, preferences, extension pairing, privacy, and support. Optional routes can handle pairing, rules refresh, feedback, privacy export, and admin operations. They authorize the caller, validate input, and return stable contracts. The extension never submits reward rates as truth, and local recommendation remains safe when a service is unavailable.

Pure recommendation logic

The engine has no framework dependency and no browser API dependency. It receives a query, wallet state, preferences, context, and published ruleset. Published reward knowledge

Reward rules are source-backed data. A ruleset needs schema validation, verification state, effective dates, review dates, a changelog, a publish step, and rollback. Testing and release quality

Cardnal's most dangerous bugs are plausible wrong answers. Testing must cover correctness, privacy, data freshness, and user comprehension together.

Engine tests

  • Normalize hostnames and reject lookalike domains.
  • Apply merchant rules before category rules.
  • Honor exclusions, caps, membership, and effective dates.
  • Rank cash against cash and each points currency separately.
  • Return deterministic ties and tiebreakers.
  • Return no rule rather than treating missing data as zero.
  • Make every successful recommendation explanation non-null.
  • Suppress dollar deltas when totals or currencies are unsafe.

Product tests

  • Sign in and sign out on desktop and mobile widths.
  • User A cannot read or alter user B data.
  • One-card, no-card, stale, unknown, and error states are designed.
  • Card search distinguishes similar products and annual fees.
  • Feedback writes only the allowed event shape.
  • Deletion and export behave as described.
  • Keyboard focus and reduced motion remain usable.
  • Unsupported merchant requests reach a review queue.
Release blocker: the current extension ruleset explicitly marks all seeded rates as unverified. No external pilot should rely on those rates until the issuer sources are checked and the verification metadata is recorded.

  • Wrong merchant classification
  • Wrong card or product match
  • Wrong reward rate or unit
  • Rule is stale or missing a condition

  • Setup or search friction
  • Unsupported merchant request

Public launch checklist

Public launch is a coordinated product, data, infrastructure, legal, and support release.

Product and data

  • Supported card and merchant matrix published internally
  • All exposed rules verified and reviewed
  • Freshness and stale behavior tested
  • Unknown, tie, no-card, and error states complete
  • Explanation and source links are understandable
  • Feedback and merchant request workflows work
  • Known limitations are visible to users

Infrastructure and security

  • Production migrations reviewed and applied
  • Development and production keys separated
  • RLS tests pass for every user-owned table
  • Service-role key is server-only
  • Rate limits and error logging are active
  • Backups and restore test completed
  • Rollback and incident runbook tested

Company operations

  • Support inbox and response owner assigned
  • Rules owner and review cadence assigned
  • Two authorized people can recover accounts
  • Vercel and GitHub deployment identity documented
  • Domain, email, MX, SPF, DKIM, and DMARC checked
  • Beta feedback reviewed and unresolved risks accepted

Legal and trust

  • Privacy policy reviewed and published
  • Terms reviewed and published
  • Rewards disclaimer is visible
  • No financial advice or guaranteed savings language
  • Data deletion and export behavior documented
  • Issuer and affiliate relationship boundaries reviewed

Interview waitlist users, capture real purchase questions, choose the first supported matrix, and approve scope gates.

Engineering

Create the web companion and extension repositories, establish development Supabase, build account, wallet, pairing, RLS, and the MV3 foundation.

Rules operation

Verify the seed cards and merchants against issuer sources, record dates and owners, and define the correction cadence.

Design

Turn the approved user map into responsive screens, edge states, explanation surfaces, and accessible interaction states.

Beta operation

Invite a small cohort, observe behavior, triage feedback weekly, and decide whether the product earns expansion.

Launch operation

Prepare privacy, terms, monitoring, support, backups, rollback, and the known-limitations message.

Near-term sequence: user conversations to verified catalog to MV3 extension foundation to popup recommendation to private extension beta to checkout evidence to public extension launch.

The goal is not to build everything.

The goal is to earn the right to build the next thing by proving that a trusted, explainable recommendation changes what people do before they pay.

Cardnal's north star: repeat, informed purchase decisions using cards people already own.
